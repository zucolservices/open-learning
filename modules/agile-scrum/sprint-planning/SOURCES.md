# Sources (fact-checked 2026-09-28, before building)

Full notes: scratchpad `agile/m06-facts.md`.

- The Scrum Guide (November 2020), © Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland, CC BY-SA 4.0: the Sprint ("During the Sprint: No changes are made that would endanger the Sprint Goal; Quality does not decrease; … Scope may be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner as more is learned."); Sprint Planning's three topics and key sentences; "The Sprint Goal must be finalized prior to the end of Sprint Planning."; past performance, capacity and DoD sentence; timebox; Sprint Goal commitment ("Although the Sprint Goal is a commitment by the Developers, it provides flexibility…"); negotiating scope "without affecting the Sprint Goal".
- Scrum Guide revisions: 2010 "The Team has committed to a Sprint Goal, and to these Product Backlog items"; 2011 "Development Teams do not commit to completing the work… The Development Team creates a forecast…" (scrumguides.org revisions page).
- Ron Jeffries, "Story Points Revisited" (23 May 2019): "I like to say that I may have invented story points, and if I did, I'm sorry now." (C3 attribution not claimed.)
- Velocity: Scrum.org glossary ("an optional, but often used, indication…"); not in the Scrum Guide.
- "Yesterday's weather": named by Kent Beck and Martin Fowler in Planning Extreme Programming (2000), per Fowler.
- Capacity from recent actuals, trimmed for absences: Henrik Kniberg, Scrum and XP from the Trenches, 2nd ed. (2015), which drops the earlier "focus factor". Support duty treated as ordinary reduced capacity.
- The team, backlog and numbers are illustrative.

# Sources (fact-checked 2026-09-28, before building)

Full notes: scratchpad `agile/m21-facts.md`.

- Goodhart (1975, RBA Papers in Monetary Economics; secondary for exact wording); Hoskin (1996); Marilyn Strathern, "'Improving ratings': audit in the British University system", European Review 5(3), 1997: "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."
- Martin Fowler, "XpVelocity" (2013): "it's stupid to compare teams based on their velocities". Ron Jeffries, "Story Points Revisited" (23 May 2019). The 2020 Scrum Guide doesn't mention velocity or story points.
- Dave West, Forrester, "Water-Scrum-Fall Is The Reality Of Agile For Most Organizations Today" (26 July 2011).
- Scrum.org on Zombie Scrum: "looks like Scrum from a distance but lacks a beating heart"; four symptoms. Verwijs, Schartau & Overeem, _The Zombie Scrum Survival Guide_ (2020).
- Stefan Wolpers, "The Hardening Sprint Fallacy", Scrum.org blog (2021): "There is no such thing as a hardening Sprint in Scrum."
- Mark Levison, Agile Pain Relief glossary: "Scrummerfall" (mini-waterfalls).
- Ron Jeffries, "Dark Scrum" (8 September 2016). Martin Fowler, "Flaccid Scrum" (29 January 2009): "progress is slow because the code base is a mess". Scrum.org, "ScrumBut".
- Scrum Guide 2020: Sprint Backlog "updated throughout the Sprint as more is learned"; "Changing the core design or ideas of Scrum… covers up problems and limits the benefits of Scrum". Scrum Guide 2011: "commitment" replaced by "forecast".
- Deliberately excluded: the Engprax "268%" claim (self-reported, commissioned, not peer reviewed).
- Teams, symptoms and chart numbers are illustrative.

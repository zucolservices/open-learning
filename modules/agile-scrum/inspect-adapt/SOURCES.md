# Sources (fact-checked 2026-09-28, before building)

Full notes: scratchpad `agile/m03-facts.md`.

- 2020 Scrum Guide (scrumguides.org): empiricism and lean thinking; the three pillars and their definitions ("Transparency enables inspection. Inspection without transparency is misleading and wasteful."; "Inspection enables adaptation. Inspection without adaptation is considered pointless."; "The adjustment must be made as soon as possible to minimize further deviation."; "A Scrum Team is expected to adapt the moment it learns anything new through inspection."); "Each event in Scrum is a formal opportunity to inspect and adapt Scrum artifacts."; "Shorter Sprints can be employed to generate more learning cycles…".
- Kent Beck, Extreme Programming Explained, 1st ed. (1999), ch. 6 "Learning to Drive": his mother's lesson ("Driving is not about getting the car going in the right direction…") and "software development is like steering".
- Schwaber & Beedle, Agile Software Development with Scrum (2001), ch. 1 (InformIT excerpt): the 1995 DuPont visit; defined vs empirical process control. Ogunnaike & Ray, Process Dynamics, Modeling, and Control (OUP, 1994).
- R. Moen, "Foundation and History of the PDSA Cycle" (deming.org): Shewhart's 1939 cycle; Deming's PDSA. Stated as an echo, not Scrum's origin.
- Deliberately omitted: OODA loop (no primary link to Scrum), Cone of Uncertainty (contested).

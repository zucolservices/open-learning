# Sources (fact-checked 2026-09-28, before building)

Full notes: scratchpad `agile/m01-facts.md`.

- Royce, "Managing the Development of Large Software Systems" (1970): "I believe in this concept, but the implementation described above is risky and invites failure"; added "do it twice" and customer involvement; the word "waterfall" does not appear. "Waterfall" in print by 1976 (Bell & Thayer) [secondary].
- Humphrey, A Discipline for Software Engineering (1995): "For a new software system, the requirements will not be completely known until after the users have used it" [secondary for the page].
- Boehm & Basili (2001): post-delivery fixes "often 100 times more expensive" for large systems, "more like 5:1" for small ones; Menzies et al. (2017) found no consistent delayed-issue effect. Stated here as direction, not law.
- Larman & Basili, "Iterative and Incremental Development: A Brief History" (IEEE Computer, 2003): Project Mercury's half-day time-boxed iterations.
- Agile Manifesto principle 3 (agilemanifesto.org/principles.html).
- PMI (PMBOK 7): predictive approaches suit requirements that "can be defined, collected, and analyzed at the start".
- FBI Virtual Case File: "flash cutover"; abandoned 2005 after ~$170M; IEEE Spectrum "constantly changing blueprints"; Sentinel went agile in-house Oct 2010, deployed July 2012 (DOJ OIG). Not claimed "under budget".
- HealthCare.gov (Oct 2013): no end-to-end testing, "the launch itself" as test, outages within two hours (HHS OIG, GAO); main contractor nominally agile, so not framed as a waterfall failure.
- GST network (1 July 2017): CAG found failures in "not just system design but its testing… before a pan-India roll out"; invoice-matching returns put on hold.
- UPI: piloted with 21 banks in April 2016, public launch August 2016.
- The simulation is illustrative (see `model.ts`); the portal and its surprises are fictional.

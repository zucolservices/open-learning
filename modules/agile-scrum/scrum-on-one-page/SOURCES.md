# Sources (fact-checked 2026-09-28, before building)

Full notes: scratchpad `agile/m04-facts.md`.

- The Scrum Guide (November 2020), scrumguides.org, © 2020 Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland, CC BY-SA 4.0: definition; Scrum Team ("typically 10 or fewer", "no sub-teams or hierarchies", accountabilities); purposes of each accountability, event and artifact (quoted verbatim in `parts.ts`); timeboxes (Sprint one month or less; Planning 8h, Review 4h, Retrospective 3h for a one-month Sprint, "usually shorter" for shorter Sprints; Daily Scrum 15 min); commitments; the five values; "purposefully incomplete"; "the result is not Scrum". Terms absent from the guide (story points, velocity, user stories, Definition of Ready, two-week Sprints) confirmed by full-text search.
- Takeuchi & Nonaka, "The New New Product Development Game", Harvard Business Review, Jan 1986: "a holistic or 'rugby' approach—where a team tries to go the distance as a unit, passing the ball back and forth—may better serve today's competitive requirements."
- Sutherland, "Lessons Learned from the First Scrum" (2004): first applied at Easel Corporation, 1993. Scrum presented publicly at OOPSLA 1995 (Schwaber's paper; the Scrum Guide says the two "first co-presented Scrum at the OOPSLA Conference in 1995").

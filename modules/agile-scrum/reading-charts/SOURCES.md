# Sources (fact-checked 2026-09-28, before building)

Full notes: scratchpad `agile/m14-facts.md`.

- Agile Alliance glossary: burndown ("the quantity of work remaining… and the time elapsed"); Schwaber introduced it around 2000. Scrum Guide 2010 required burndowns; 2020 lists "burn-downs, burn-ups, or cumulative flows" as practices that "do not replace the importance of empiricism".
- Atlassian: burndown ideal line; "plateaus indicate blockers, late drops suggest batch updates or end-loaded work, and upward jumps often mean scope was added"; burnup "plotting completed work against total scope over time".
- ActionableAgile help (Vacanti) and Scrum.org's Kanban Guide for Scrum Teams (2021): a CFD shows WIP, "approximate average Cycle Time and average Throughput"; a bulging band means work "entering that workflow stage but not moving out as fast". Approximation holds only for stable flow.
- ActionableAgile: scatterplot "Each dot represents a work item that was completed"; percentile reading ("85% of the time our work finishes in… or less"). Aging items belong on a Work Item Age chart, not the scatterplot.
- Rally (Broadcom) on CFD stair steps (batching) and flat lines.
- Monte Carlo: Daniel Vacanti (2015, 2020); Troy Magennis (Focused Objective).
- Burndown and burnup data are illustrative; the CFD and scatterplot use `../kanban-wip/model`.

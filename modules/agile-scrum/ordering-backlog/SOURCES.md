# Sources (fact-checked 2026-09-28, before building)

Full notes: scratchpad `agile/m12-facts.md`.

- Scrum Guide 2020 ("an emergent, ordered list"); revisions page (ordered, not prioritized, 2011); 2011 guide ("often ordered by value, risk, priority, and necessity").
- Donald Reinertsen, The Principles of Product Development Flow (2009): E3 "If you only quantify one thing, quantify the cost of delay."; F17 "When job durations and delay costs are not homogeneous, use WSJF."
- Black Swan Farming (Joshua Arnold): CD3 definition and worked example (FIFO $69,000 vs CD3 $27,000); urgency profiles (not used by name). Arnold's critique of SAFe's additive relative WSJF noted neutrally.
- SAFe: WSJF "estimated as the relative cost of delay divided by the relative job duration".
- Agile Business Consortium (DSDM): MoSCoW categories incl. "Won't Have this time"; "not to exceed 60% Must Have effort". Dai Clegg & Barker, Case Method Fast-Track (1994).
- Mike Cohn: ordering factors value, cost, learning, risk, dependencies; "If something is risky and you need to do it, do it early."
- HiPPO: coined at Intuit/Microsoft (2006), popularised by Avinash Kaushik.
- Items, values and durations are illustrative.

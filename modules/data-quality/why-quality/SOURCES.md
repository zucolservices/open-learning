# Sources: Why data quality matters (fact-checked 2026-10-04)

- R. Y. Wang and D. M. Strong, "Beyond Accuracy: What Data Quality Means to Data Consumers", Journal of Management Information Systems 12(4), 1996 ("data that are fit for use by data consumers", building on Juran's fitness for use).
- Gartner, "How to Improve Your Data Quality", 14 Jul 2021 ($12.9 million average annual cost, from 2020 research; self-reported).
- T. C. Redman, "Bad Data Costs the U.S. $3 Trillion Per Year", HBR, 22 Sep 2016 (hidden data factories; IBM's $3.1 trillion estimate).
- T. Nagle, T. C. Redman, D. Sammon, "Only 3% of Companies' Data Meets Basic Quality Standards", HBR, 11 Sep 2017 (rule of ten; 47% of new records with a critical error; 75 executives).
- 1-10-100 rule of thumb, usually credited to G. Labovitz and Y. S. Chang (1992); not empirical.
- Unity Q1 2022 prepared remarks, 10 May 2022, and CNBC, 11 May 2022; Korea Times, 12 Aug 2026, and Korea Herald, April 2018 (Samsung Securities); Equifax statement, 2 Aug 2022.
